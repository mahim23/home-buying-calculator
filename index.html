Amortisation schedule

  • Interest is computed monthly on the reducing balance (Irish lenders accrue daily and charge monthly; the difference is a few euro a month).
  • When the fixed period ends, the monthly payment is recalculated on the remaining balance over the remaining term at the follow-on rate.
  • Selling inside a fixed-rate period can trigger a break fee (BOI charges it only if wholesale rates have fallen since you fixed); not modelled.
  • No capital gains tax applies when selling your principal private residence in Ireland.
  • Extra monthly income is treated as fixed and tax-free; rent-a-room relief covers up to €14,000/yr in your primary residence, but the full amount becomes taxable if you exceed the threshold.
  • Section 2 taxes all gains at the flat rate you enter; the €1,270/yr CGT exemption on shares and the 8-year deemed-disposal rule on ETFs are not modelled. No capital gains tax applies in Section 1 since it is your principal private residence.
  • Rent in Section 2 is held flat (RPZ rules cap in-tenancy increases at ~2%/yr); rising rent would favour buying.
  • Monthly invested amounts are end-of-month contributions compounding at the monthly equivalent of the annual return; the contributions themselves come back when you cash out, so only their after-tax gains move the net result.
  • Running costs cover management fees, LPT, and insurances as a flat annual figure; large one-off repairs, room-vacancy gaps, and mortgage overpayments are not modelled.
